Anterograde Amnesia: A Complete Explanation
By- Rahul Bit (32)
Anterograde amnesia is a memory disorder in which a person cannot form new long-term memories after the brain injury or illness begins. Memories from before the injury are often preserved, but new information is quickly forgotten.


What Causes Anterograde Amnesia? 

The most common causes include:
Damage to the hippocampus (the brain's memory center)
Traumatic brain injury (head injury)
Stroke
Brain surgery
Brain infections (encephalitis)
Alzheimer's disease (especially early stages)
Lack of vitamin B1 (Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome)
Certain medications or heavy alcohol use

Symptoms

People with anterograde amnesia usually:
Forget conversations within minutes or hours
Ask the same question repeatedly
Forget names of new people
Cannot remember recent events
Need constant reminders
Remember older memories well
Can often learn some motor skills (like riding a bicycle) without remembering practicing
